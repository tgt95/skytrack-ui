# Marketing, graphic design, and art direction

## Brief and reference

Establish audience, primary message, desired action, and brand personality from existing material. Ask only for missing information that affects the outcome. In YOLO, infer nonessential details and state assumptions; obtain essential missing facts rather than fabricating product claims.

Before designing a marketing page, research its information architecture from the brief, existing site/navigation and related campaign or product material. Organize sections as a narrative for the target audience, primary message, and intended action; do not force product data-object grouping onto marketing pages. In STRICT, include this narrative outline in the review checkpoint. In YOLO, prepare it before implementation and surface only material assumptions or trade-offs. If project evidence is missing or conflicting, no suitable pattern exists, or plausible page structures would materially change narrative, section order, or conversion path, research relevant external page patterns for that assignment, cite useful sources, and compare them with the brand and brief.

Establish whether a reference is a faithful reproduction target or inspiration. If unclear, propose an interpretation in STRICT; state and use a reasonable interpretation in YOLO. Honor an explicit interpretation in the initial prompt without asking again.

## Direction selection

In STRICT, when no specific reference already settles the direction, propose 2–3 meaningfully different directions and choose with the Owner before implementation. Normally include a safe direction, a distinctive direction, and an experimental direction, each grounded in the brief. Present each with a moodboard/concept image and a short account of typography, composition, palette, imagery, and motion. Do not use minor color swaps as separate directions.

In YOLO, assess these alternatives internally, select the most appropriate direction, and proceed without requiring a moodboard approval. Produce concept imagery only when it materially supports the deliverable.

Use existing brand/product assets first. When assets are insufficient, propose generation or a suitable source in STRICT. In YOLO choose and create/source assets within the authorized resources. Respect the project's available image tools and asset requirements. Clearly distinguish genuine product imagery from conceptual illustration.

For graphics, choose a suitable format/aspect ratio based on intended use and disclose it for later adjustment. Explicit brief dimensions, content requirements, and delivery constraints take priority. Do not require a format interview when a reasonable choice is available.

## Motion and execution

Prominent motion is on by default for marketing sites: choose suitable reveal, parallax, or scroll animation in service of the selected concept. Ask non-blockingly whether the Owner wants motion reduced/disabled; absent a response, proceed with the default. An explicit preference already in the prompt settles the choice. Do not require all effects on every page or animate static graphic exports.

Keep text readable, controls usable, layout stable, and support `prefers-reduced-motion`. Use available, project-approved motion tools; the skill does not mandate installing a particular library.

After direction selection, continue implementing and refining autonomously in YOLO. In STRICT, continue routine polish within the approved slice but wait before the next dependent checkpoint. Escalate material changes to the agreed concept, core message, or scope according to the selected mode and existing authority.

## Quality review

Before handoff compare with the brief and chosen direction. Review hierarchy, typography, spacing, imagery quality/cropping, motion, and consistency; repair weak spots. For code, apply implementation checks including desktop/mobile and principal interactions. For graphics, inspect the actual rendered/exported artifact at its intended size for clipping, legibility, and composition.

Report actual evidence and unresolved limits. Do not substitute claims of “premium” or “modern” for observable design decisions and inspected output.
